## 1. Malaysian law and regulators (highest risk for our ads)
Our brands are men's health (SS Clinic: clinic services; Dr. MIT Ring, MENSS, REJAL: products). Malaysian rules can be stricter than Meta's, and Meta also requires ads to follow local law.

**Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act 1956 (Act 290)**, source: pharmacy.moh.gov.my, Act 290 text (revised 1983, amendments to 1990, check for later changes):
- s.3(1)(c): no advertisement promoting an article as a medicine, appliance or remedy for "improving the sexual function or sexual performance of human beings".
- Schedule items 17, 18, 20: frigidity; "impairment of sexual function or impotency"; "nervous debility, or other complaint or infirmity, arising from or relating to sexual intercourse".
- s.4A: no advertisement referring to a skill or service relating to the treatment, prevention or diagnosis of an ailment that invites people to seek the advertiser's advice.
- s.4B: advertisements for other medicines need Medicine Advertisements Board (MAB) approval.
- s.5 penalties: first conviction up to RM 3,000 or 1 year; later up to RM 5,000 or 2 years, or both.

**MAB Policy and Decision (Services), 15 May 2025** (read directly):
- MAB 12/2007, amended 1/2025: clinic-service advertisements may NOT use patients receiving treatment or professionals (doctors, dentists, pharmacists). Healthcare providers and patients portrayed by models or actors are allowed, with this statement on screen: "This image/video is for illustrative purposes only. It does not represent an actual medical event, procedure, or interaction between healthcare professional and patient."
- Same policy: celebrities (local or international celebrities, athletes, models) only at MAB's discretion, case by case, and never misleading about the service. Treat KOLs and influencers as celebrities.
- MAB 4/2016, amended 2/2019: ONE advertisement format or social media posting per application. Every clinic ad variant needs its own approval (KKLIU serial).
- MAB 2/2019: no before and after pictures.
- MAB 5/2011: statistical claims should be supported by Malaysian data unless not available.
- MAB 2/2017, amended 1/2023: a social media logo or link in an ad only if the KKLIU number of that post is given in the application.

**MAB guidelines for advertising healthcare services** (MAB 6/2011 document, older; confirm the current version with MAB):
- Patient testimonials may not be published (5.3).
- No comparisons between facilities (5.1) and no superlatives such as "the best" or "state-of-the-art" (5.2).
- Content must be accurate, substantiable and not exaggerated, false, misleading or deceptive (2.2).
- No financial inducements, sales promotions or fee discounts (7.0).
- Practitioner name, qualifications and specialty may be listed; promoting individual skill or experience may not (4.2).

**MMC Guideline V2/2025, The Dissemination of Information by Medical Professionals including on Social Media** (endorsed 19 Aug 2025, replaces MMC Guideline 001/2006; read directly):
- Definition: "advertising" is read in the broadest sense, covering anything that can fairly be regarded as for obtaining patients or promoting the doctor's professional advantage, or that appears to be for those purposes.
- 3.2 (Code of Professional Conduct 2019, 4.1): doctors must not advertise directly or indirectly to obtain patients, must not canvass, and must not be associated with or employed by those who do.
- 5.4: doctors MAY discuss healthcare topics on mass and social media if the discussion is evidence-based, scientific and rational.
- 5.5.4: in public talks, doctors should be cautious about promotional activity, including directing people to the practitioner's place of practice.
- 2.6: claiming to be the best or the only one, or "firsts" and "breakthroughs", is unethical.
- 5.9: direct association with a company selling something claimed to prevent or treat disease, presented in a way that encourages self-diagnosis or self-medication, is not allowed.
- 5.14: doctors are discouraged from promoting or endorsing health-related products on any media; if they do, the product must be evidence-based and approved by the regulator (NPRA or Medical Device Authority), with no unsubstantiated or cure claims.
- 6.3: doctors must clearly identify themselves as doctors when discussing health topics online.
- 6.5: direct or indirect advertising for personal gain, such as patient solicitation or self-promotion, is not appropriate on social media.

**What this means for our work (flag to the human before any batch for Malaysia):**
- SS Clinic runs in two content modes (section 1a). A real doctor never appears in a clinic advertisement.
- A real doctor's video must not end with a call to visit SS Clinic ("kita check dan rawat di SS Clinic", "datang ke SS Clinic"). That turns education into patient solicitation (MMC 3.2, 6.5; Act 290 s.4A) and makes the video a clinic ad that may not show a real doctor (MAB 12/2007).
- Changing the doctor's clothes does not change any of this. The rules follow who he is and what the video does. He must still be identified as a doctor (MMC 6.3).
- Quote popups and WhatsApp proof screenshots are patient testimonials. Never on clinic content.
- Offer cards with discounts or promotions count as inducements for clinic services.
- Any claim that a product or service improves sexual function or performance, or treats impotence, is a legal risk under Act 290, whatever Meta allows. Hooks like "besarkan zakar?" and "morning wood = tak serius?" need human sign-off.
- Doctors in PRODUCT ads (for example Dr Amir for Dr. MIT Ring): MMC 5.14 and 5.9 put the risk on the doctor's registration. The brand decision of 2026-10-16 still stands, but the doctor and an adviser should confirm it.
- Whether MAB approval is needed, and for which brands (product vs service), is a decision for the brand owner. Claude does not decide it.

### 1a. SS Clinic content modes (Head of Creative decision 2026-10-21)
**Educational (default)**: real SS Clinic doctor, organic posts.
- The doctor is identified on screen (lower third with name and title). Plain clothes or uniform are both fine.
- Evidence-based teaching only. No superlatives, no "pakar terbaik", no promoting his skill or experience.
- Close with a neutral line such as "Jumpa doktor untuk pemeriksaan jika ada simptom". No line that sends viewers to SS Clinic, no booking link, no price, offer or testimonial.
- Open item: whether posting from the SS Clinic page or showing the clinic logo already counts as advertising. Ask the adviser or MAB in writing.

**KOL ad**: paid clinic advertisement presented by a KOL, never by a real doctor.
- Each posting needs its own KKLIU approval before it runs. The KKLIU number goes in the spec.
- The KOL may speak about the premises and the experience of the visit (cleanliness, staff explaining clearly). No testimonial about treatment results or a doctor's skill.
- If anyone portrays a doctor or patient, the MAB illustrative statement (above) is on screen.
- No discounts, inducements, before/after, superlatives or countdowns.

## 2. Meta rules (verify on the live pages)
Pages to check: transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards (Restricted goods and services: Health and wellness; Content-specific restrictions: Adult products or services; Deceptive content: Unrealistic outcomes; Objectionable content: Personal attributes).

**Unrealistic outcomes** (read directly from Meta, no page date shown): health ads may not claim to cure, heal or eliminate incurable diseases or terminal illnesses; specific results need qualifying or disclaimer language; sensational click-bait wording is not allowed; example of a banned claim: "Lose 5% of your body weight in a month!".

**Personal attributes** (stackmatix.com summary, page dated 2026-09-08): copy, images and video text must not state or imply that the viewer has a medical condition. Second-person "you have / are you suffering from ..." copy is the trigger. Write about the offer or about "people who want X" instead.

**Sexual health** (femtechinsider.com, dated 2022-10-18, and aimerce.ai summary dated 2026-08-24):
- Allowed: sexual health, wellness and reproductive products and services, for example erectile dysfunction products and premature ejaculation treatments, contraception, sex education.
- Not allowed: content or products that focus on sexual pleasure, arousal or sexual enhancement.
- Ads in this category must be targeted at 18+.
- Claude's note: "besarkan" (enlargement) and performance framing read as enhancement, which is a review risk.

**Health and wellness general** (adm summary dated 2026-08-27; aimerce summary dated 2026-08-24):
- No negative statements about physical appearance, and no close-ups that pinch or highlight body parts.
- Before/after images are restricted for weight loss, and allowed only for certain cosmetic procedures, for adults. For sexual or men's health, treat before/after as not allowed.
- Dietary, health, weight-loss and weight-gain products must be targeted 18+.
- Online pharmacies and telehealth need LegitScript certification.
- Accounts tied to health conditions can have restricted conversion events for optimization, and sensitive custom audiences or lookalikes can be disabled. A media buyer should check this.

**Adult nudity and sexual content**: no nudity or suggestive imagery. Our rules already say fruit metaphors only and no anatomy imagery.

**Disguised wording**: do not use creative spelling, emoji swaps or code words to get flagged terms past platform review. It is policy evasion and risks ad rejection and account action. If a line is flagged, choose a different real line.

## 3. Rules applied in the pipeline (from the above and earlier decisions)
- Fruit metaphors only; no anatomy imagery, no pills, no distressed people. No inflation or pressure metaphors (balloons, engines, pumps) for devices.
- No second-person condition statements in headlines, captions or hooks ("Anda ada masalah ...", "Are you suffering ...").
- No guarantees, cure claims, sensational wording, invented statistics, or specific results without a disclaimer the human approves.
- No before/after imagery.
- No sexual pleasure, arousal or enhancement framing.
- Testimonials and review popups: real text only, names blurred, and never on SS Clinic content.
- SS Clinic: Educational mode has no clinic CTA; KOL mode needs a KKLIU number per posting (section 1a).
- Customer names in WhatsApp proof screenshots must be blurred.
- Disclaimers keep the bottom 768 px clear on Reels (see `safe-zones.md`).
- Ads for sexual health products target 18+ (media buyer sets this).
